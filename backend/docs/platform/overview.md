# Overview & Core Concepts

Orbit is the central catalog and scoring system for application security, provided to your company as part of Hearst's **Exposure Management** service. It's not a scanner — it's where the security metadata for everything you run lives, always current, so you always know what exists, how secure it is, and where it stands against policy.

## The building blocks

| | |
|---|---|
| **Company** | The business your account belongs to. Owns every application, product, and domain in Orbit. **[Read more about Companies →](/docs/companies-and-team)** |
| **Application** | A website, service, or internal tool your company runs — the most detailed record in Orbit. **[Read more about Applications →](/docs/applications)** |
| **Product** | A group of applications that together deliver one business capability, with a rolled-up security score of its own. **[Read more about Products →](/docs/products)** |
| **Domain** | A hosting domain where your applications are actually served, with its own DNS and web-reachability history. **[Read more about Domains →](/docs/domains)** |
| **Division** | A group of Companies, usually mirroring how the business is organized. Mainly relevant for setting policy once across a whole business unit. |
| **Policy** | A set of security requirements, made up of individual Controls, that your applications are checked against automatically. **[Read more about Policies & Compliance →](/docs/policies-and-samm)** |

## How posture is measured

Orbit tracks two different things, and it's worth keeping them apart:

- **Score** — a numeric measure of an application's (or product's) security tooling and testing coverage. **[Read more about how it's calculated →](/docs/scoring-methodology)**
- **Policy compliance** — a pass/fail check against your company's actual security requirements.

Both come from the same catalog data, but answer different questions. **[See where to view both →](/docs/dashboards)**
